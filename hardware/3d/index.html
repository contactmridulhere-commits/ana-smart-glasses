What it does

Every feature, and where it lives

Grouped by job. The monospace line under each item names the board and the part references, so you can find it in the 3D view, the schematic PDFs and the BOM.

Heads-up display

  • 0.96″ 128 × 64 SSD1306 OLED in a pod above the right lens, reflected to your eye by a 45° beam splitter.
    OLED module on J4 · 7 wires · SPI CLK 4, MOSI 23, CS 15, DC 26, RST 27
  • Shows the time and date, temperature, heart rate and SpO₂, battery % or USB/charging state, a flashlight marker and a footer line.
    firmware readSlowSensors() + drawScreen()
  • Drawn upside down in firmware so it reads correctly after the reflection.
    display.setRotation(2)
  • 33 Ω series resistors on the SPI clock and data lines tame ringing on the 7-wire cable.
    ana-main R6, R7

Brain and radio

  • ESP32-U4WDH: dual-core 240 MHz ESP32 with 4 MB flash inside the package, soldered straight to the board.
    ana-main U1 · 5 × 5 mm QFN-48
  • Bluetooth Classic serial to your phone; set the time with T: dd/mm/yyyy hh:mm.
    SerialBT "Genius_MRIDUL_G"
  • 2.4 GHz ceramic chip antenna at the very end of the ear tip, with a tunable CLC match next to the chip.
    ana-main AE1, C15, L2, C16, R8 (C17, C18 not fitted)
  • 40 MHz ±10 ppm crystal with 22 pF loads; EN RC delay and boot strap pull-ups as in Espressif's reference design.
    ana-main Y1, C13, C14, R2, C12, R3

Sensing

  • Heart rate and SpO₂ from a MAX30102 on the skin side of the right temple, read at 400 samples/s, averaged over 5 beats.
    ana-main U4 · I²C 0x57 · INT on GPIO 5 · 1.8 V core LDO U5
  • Temperature and humidity from an SHT40 (±0.2 °C) on the top edge, away from the regulator and the ESP32.
    ana-main U6 · I²C 0x44 · no copper pour around it
  • Battery voltage through a 100 k / 100 k divider; above 4.4 V the firmware knows USB is plugged in.
    ana-main R9, R10, C23 → GPIO 33
  • Charging status from the TP4056, isolated by a Schottky diode so a switched-off ESP32 is never back-powered.
    ana-power D3 → link wire 6 → GPIO 13

Touch and lights

  • Capacitive touch pad (10 × 4 mm copper) on the outer face of the right temple; tap through up to 2 mm of plastic.
    ana-main E1 + TTP223 U7 (toggle mode)
  • Each tap toggles the flashlight: one MOSFET switches the LEDs on both temples, and the ESP32 sees it on GPIO 14 and shows * on the HUD.
    ana-main Q2 (AO3400A), R20 · LED_K over link wire 2
  • White side-emitting LEDs at both hinge ends, plus pads for brighter LEDs at the frame corners (68 Ω already fitted).
    D1 / D5, R21 / R11 · EXT_LED pads J3 / J4 with R22 / R12
  • Red (charging) and green (full) indicator LEDs on the outer face of the left temple.
    ana-power D1, D2

Power

  • USB-C on the bottom edge of the left temple; 5.1 kΩ CC resistors so any USB-C charger or C-to-C cable gives 5 V, and ESD protection on the data lines.
    ana-power J1, R1, R2, U1 (USBLC6)
  • TP4056 linear charger set to 44 mA (0.7 C for the 60 mAh cell).
    ana-power U2, R3 = 27 kΩ
  • Cell protection: over-charge 4.3 V, over-discharge 2.4 V, over-current and short-circuit cut-off.
    ana-power U3 (DW01A) + Q1 (FS8205A)
  • Power path: the glasses run from USB while it is plugged in, so the charger sees only the cell and stops at full.
    ana-power D4 (B5819WS), Q2 (AO3401A), R8
  • POWER slide switch drives a P-MOSFET; the tiny switch only carries gate current.
    ana-power SW1 → Q3, R9
  • 3.3 V 600 mA LDO on the right temple, 1.8 V LDO for the MAX30102 core.
    ana-main U2 (AP2112K), U5 (XC6206)

Flashing and audio

  • USB-C flashing like a devkit: CP2102N USB-serial plus DTR/RTS auto-reset; the Arduino IDE uploads with one click.
    ana-power U4 (CP2102N), Q5 (MMDT3904), R13–R17, C8–C10
  • Only RX, TX, EN and IO0 cross the frame; USB data stays on the left board.
    link wires 4, 5, 7, 8
  • BT slide switch powers your Bluetooth headset module from the protected cell (never from 5 V).
    ana-power SW2 → Q4, R10, C7 · BT_HEADSET pads J2
  • Bring-up pads on the ear tip for 3.3 V, GND, TX and RX.
    ana-main J2

Boards and build

  • Two 4-layer, 0.8 mm boards cut to the temple shape (straight 104.5 mm, then 39.5 mm bent 30° down behind the ear).
    config/temple.json · 1132 mm² each
  • Solid ground plane on layer 2, all parts on one side, JLCPCB-assembled (55 + 44 parts).
    fab/ana-main, fab/ana-power
  • Checks: 0 ERC, 0 DRC errors, 0 unconnected, schematic and board match on both.
    drc.json / erc.json
  • 8-wire frame link over the brow; 7-wire OLED cable; plated wire holes at 1.5 mm pitch.
    J1 (right) ↔ J3 (left)
Weight

About 43 g on your face

Board and cover figures are computed from the real outlines; parts come from datasheet or typical masses. The frame depends on what you make it from, so both common options are shown. Your Bluetooth headset module is not included.

ItemBasisGrams

Range: about 37 g with a 3D-printed front, thin lenses and 0.8 mm cover walls, up to about 48 g with a thick acetate front and glass lenses. Everyday acetate glasses weigh roughly 25–35 g. The right side (display pod, OLED and beam splitter) is about 3 g heavier than the left (battery), so the Bluetooth module hanging at the left ear evens the balance.
